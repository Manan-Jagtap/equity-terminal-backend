# Equity Terminal — Backend Audit (June 2026)

> Scope: a full read of the backend codebase (canonical repo at HEAD `9ef501c`), cross-checked against the **live** Railway API. The goal of this document is to tell you, honestly, what exists, what works, what is broken, what is dead, and the shortest path to a genuinely best-in-class, *independent* valuation terminal.
>
> Direction set by you: (1) deliver this written audit first; (2) the intrinsic value should be an **independent** model that can disagree with analysts; (3) work on the canonical repo; (4) **IndianAPI stays the primary source — harden it**, don't add a new vendor yet.

---

## 0. Executive summary

The architecture is genuinely good and the bones are better than most retail terminals: a clean layered design, a real sector-template system, and a trust/data-quality layer that refuses to print confident numbers on weak data. That last one is institutional-grade thinking that Screener/Tickertape do not do.

But the terminal is **not yet trustworthy as an independent valuation engine**, and the reasons are concrete, not vague:

1. **The DCF values companies off stale yfinance snapshots, not the 7 years of clean IndianAPI history already in your database.** The per-company assumptions (growth, margin, beta) were generated by a yfinance script that isn't even in the repo anymore. `beta` is a flat ~1.0 for everyone, so cost of equity doesn't vary by risk. This is the single biggest reason the DCF is off.
2. **The company page and the screener disagree on the same stock.** The company detail endpoint returns the raw DCF (TCS = ₹1,727, **TRIM**); the screener applies a consensus overlay and shows something else. Two intrinsics, two verdicts, same company.
3. **The consensus overlay quietly turned the "independent" DCF into a consensus tracker** — it blends 60% analyst target and clamps everything into the analyst band. Given your choice of an independent model, this has to be reworked.
4. **The financial-sector P&L — the part you called your edge — is nearly empty.** HDFC Bank is mis-templated as an NBFC, its bank P&L has only 3 populated lines, and its ROE prints as 68–92% because of a book-value bug.
5. **Real dead/broken code** is shipping: a valuation-cache script that references a table that doesn't exist, a backend Anthropic thesis module that isn't wired to anything, ~9 probe scripts and ~25 `.bak` files in the tree.

None of this is fatal. The data you need (7-year statements, analyst targets, prices) is already flowing and stored. The work is to **compute valuations from the good data you already have**, make one valuation the single source of truth, and fix the financial-sector pipeline. Detailed below.

## 1. What is genuinely strong (keep and build on)

- **Layered architecture** (`presentation → API → computation → normalization → storage → ingestion`). Clean separation; `ARCHITECTURE.md` is unusually honest and even lists its own debt.
- **Sector-template system** (`templates.py`, 8 codes) routing each company to the right P&L shape, the right ratios, and the right valuation engine (FCFF for non-financials, Residual Income for lenders). The *idea* is exactly right — you should not DCF a bank like a manufacturer.
- **Trust layer** (`data_quality.py`). Penalizes missing inputs and specifically catches the stale-price-vs-stale-fundamentals trap (the Bajaj Finance "P/E 3.4x" mirage). The engine multiplies its score into the composite and refuses a confident verdict on low-confidence data. This is the best single idea in the codebase.
- **Declarative metric registry** (`metrics.py`, 80+ ratios, each tagged by template). Clean, extensible, sector-aware.
- **IndianAPI ingester** (`ingest/indianapi_ingester.py`). Well-built: one `/stock` call yields 7 annual years of INC/BAL/CAS, corrects the share count via PAT/EPS, has an insurer fallback via `/statement`, and treats insight sections as best-effort so a flaky endpoint never breaks core statements.
- **Scheduler** (`scheduler.py`). Quota-aware, IndianAPI-only, explicitly refuses to re-run the deprecated yfinance ingesters. Daily prices + weekly full refresh, ~2,700 calls/month against a 10,000 plan. Sensible.

## 2. Critical issues (verified against the live API)

### C1 — The DCF runs on yfinance-derived assumptions, not your IndianAPI data
**Evidence:** `GET /api/companies/TCS` → assumptions `beta 1.0, ebit_margin 0.22, rev_growth 0.14, forecast_roe 0.15, terminal_roe 0.13`. These come from `bulk_ingester.derive_financial_assumptions(yf.Ticker(...).info)` (referenced by `fix_roe_sweep.py`), i.e. **yfinance** — the source you officially migrated away from. The generator (`bulk_ingester.py`) is **not in the canonical repo**; the values are frozen in production Postgres with no reproducible pipeline.

**Why it matters:** every DCF intrinsic is built on stale third-party point estimates while 7 years of clean statements sit unused in `historical_financials`. `beta` is ~1.0 for everyone, so cost of equity (`risk_free + beta·erp`) is effectively constant across a gold-loan NBFC and a consumer staple. That is not a defensible independent model.

**Fix (this is the core of the "independent DCF" work):** a new, version-controlled `derive_assumptions.py` that reads each company's stored 7-year IndianAPI history and computes, deterministically:
- revenue/EBIT/PAT growth from the actual 3–7yr trend (with sane caps),
- normalized margins from the history (not a single yfinance number),
- reinvestment from actual capex + working-capital change,
- ROE/terminal ROE from realized returns,
- **sector beta** from a small India sector-beta table (you already researched these),
- fade and terminal growth from sector + size.

Reproducible, auditable, and grounded in data you already trust.

### C2 — Company page and screener show different intrinsics/verdicts
**Evidence:** `/api/companies/TCS` (detail) returns `intrinsic 1727, verdict "TRIM"` (raw engine, no overlay). `/api/companies` (list) applies `_consensus_overlay` and ranks TCS differently. Same stock, two answers.

**Why it matters:** it's the most visible possible inconsistency. A user clicks a screener BUY and the company page shows TRIM at a lower number.

**Fix:** one valuation function, one result, used by both endpoints. Compute intrinsic + verdict once per company (ideally precomputed — see C5) and read it everywhere.

### C3 — "TRIM" still ships, and the engine still disagrees with itself on verdict scheme
**Evidence:** `engines.recommend` still emits `TRIM`; only the list endpoint normalizes it (`{"TRIM":"REDUCE"}`). The detail endpoint returns raw `TRIM`. You've repeatedly asked for TRIM to be gone.

**Fix:** delete `TRIM` at the source in `engines.py`; pick one verdict scheme (BUY/ACCUMULATE/HOLD/REDUCE/AVOID) and define it in exactly one place.

### C4 — The consensus overlay defeats the independent-DCF goal
**Evidence:** `_consensus_overlay` = `0.60·analyst_target + 0.20·fwd_PE + 0.20·DCF`, with every fundamental component **clamped into the analyst [low, high] band** and a final clamp to `[low·0.95, high·1.05]`. Net effect: intrinsic ≈ analyst consensus echoed back. The terminal structurally cannot disagree with the street.

**Why it matters:** you chose an *independent* model. This is the opposite — it was a pragmatic patch for the all-AVOID problem.

**Fix:** make the **independent DCF/RI the headline intrinsic**, and show the analyst consensus as a *separate, labeled* number next to it with the gap explained ("model sees 12% downside; street sees 9% upside"). Keep a soft sanity bound only to catch obviously broken inputs (e.g. negative terminal value), not to anchor the answer. Once C1 fixes the inputs, the DCF won't need the analyst crutch.

### C5 — `compute_valuations.py` is dead and broken
**Evidence:** it writes to `models.Valuation`, **which does not exist** in `models.py`. It would crash on first run. And `/api/companies` doesn't read a valuations table anyway — it recomputes the full DCF + technicals for every company inside the request loop (cached 5 min).

**Why it matters:** (a) dead code that looks load-bearing; (b) the live endpoint does heavy work per request, which is why it's slow/timeout-prone on cold cache.

**Fix:** either add the `Valuation` table and actually precompute (recommended — makes the list endpoint instant and removes the per-request DCF), or delete the script. Given you want one source of truth (C2), precomputing into a table is the clean answer.

### C6 — Financial-sector P&L is nearly empty and ROE is wrong (your declared edge is broken)
**Evidence:** `GET /api/companies/HDFCBANK/financials`:
- `template: "NBFC"` — HDFC **Bank** classified as an NBFC (sector "Financial Services" hits the NBFC catch-all before any bank rule).
- P&L per year contains only `pbt, tax, pat, roe`. **No `nii`, `interest_income`, `total_income`, `opex`, `provisions`, `cost_to_income`, `nim`** — because `indianapi_ingester.INC_MAP` maps Reuters fields to `revenue/ebit/pat`, never to bank line items. The NII-based template asks for data the ingester never produces.
- `roe` prints `68.6 / 82.4 / 84.3 / 92.5 / 49.4`. Bug in `financials.py`: `eq = BS.get("equity") or BS.get("net_worth")` picks the **share-capital** line (₹554cr) before **net worth** (₹2.5L cr), so ROE = PAT / share-capital. Real ROE is ~15–17%.
- Cash-flow / FCF is computed for a bank, which is conceptually meaningless.

**Fix:**
1. Classifier: add explicit bank rules *and* a name-based override (`"hdfc bank"`, `"icici bank"`, `"state bank"`, `"axis"`, `"kotak"` → BANK) before the NBFC catch-all. (You already do name-based detection on the frontend; mirror it server-side.)
2. Ingester: add a bank/NBFC mapping path that pulls `interest_income`, `interest_expense → nii`, `provisions`, `opex` from `/statement` (the insurer-fallback shape) so the financial P&L actually populates.
3. `financials.py`: ROE must use net worth — `eq = BS.net_worth or (equity+reserves)`, never share capital.
4. Suppress FCF/CF-FCF rows for financial templates.

### C7 — The taxonomy is too coarse for valuation multiples
**Evidence:** `templates.classify` collapses **autos, metals, cement, telecom, utilities** all into `MANUFACTURING`. `main.py._SCREENER_SECTOR_PE` *does* define AUTO/METAL/TELECOM/CEMENT/UTILITIES multiples — but they're **dead config**: a company's `template_code` is already `MANUFACTURING`, so the `template_code in _SCREENER_SECTOR_PE` check short-circuits and everyone gets the manufacturing P/E of 20. Maruti (trades ~26x) and Tata Steel (~10x) get the same multiple.

**Fix:** either split the taxonomy (add AUTO, METAL, CEMENT, TELECOM, UTILITIES as real templates) or — lighter — keep 8 P&L shapes but carry a separate `valuation_sector` field that drives multiples/beta/terminal independently of the P&L shape. The second is less work and probably right.

## 4. Lower-priority issues

- **No tests.** For a product whose whole pitch is "accurate numbers," there is nothing pinning valuation output per company. A 30-line pytest that asserts intrinsic/verdict for ~10 known Nifty names would catch every future regression. High value, low effort.
- **CORS `allow_origins=["*"]`.** Lock to the Vercel domain.
- **In-process caches** (`_COMPANIES_CACHE`, `_PEER_UNIV_CACHE`) are per-worker and cold on restart. Fine at current scale; the precompute table (C5) makes them unnecessary.
- **Two valuation code paths.** The onepager route in `main.py` hardcodes its own intrinsic (`ke = 0.071 + 0.86·0.085`, equity fallback `shares·200`) divorced from the engine. Fold into the single valuation function.
- **`bse_filing_date` still NULL** (BSE fractional-second timestamps unparsed) — noted in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, still open.
- **The accuracy spine (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §5) is aspirational.** No provenance-per-number, no balance-sheet tie-out, no PAT reconciliation. You asked to keep IndianAPI primary — so the near-term version of "the spine" is **validation on IndianAPI data**: does the balance sheet balance, does PAT reconcile to the CF opening line, is `borrowings − cash` sane. Flag mismatches in the UI rather than building a second data source.

## 5. What it would take to credibly rival the big terminals (focused, not boil-the-ocean)

You won't out-breadth Bloomberg. You can out-judge them on the Nifty 50 with four things they do generically and you can do excellently:

1. **An independent, defensible, reproducible valuation** (sections C1–C5). Intrinsic from your own 7-year data, shown beside consensus, with the gap explained. This is the product.
2. **Sector-correct financials that are actually populated** — especially banks/NBFCs (C6). This is the moat you already identified.
3. **A validation layer on IndianAPI data** (tie-outs + "numbers don't tie" flag). Cheap, and it's the difference between "looks authoritative" and "is."
4. **Historical valuation context** — P/E and P/B percentile bands over 5–10 years ("TCS is in the 78th percentile of its own 10-yr P/E"). You already store the history; this is a high-value, low-cost feature Screener/Tickertape lean on heavily.

Deliberately deferred: real-time/intraday, fixed income, FX, derivatives, non-Nifty breadth.

## 6. Recommended sequence

1. **Stop the bleeding (1 pass):** delete `TRIM` at source (C3); unify company-page and screener intrinsic (C2); fix bank classification + ROE bug (C6 #1, #3); clean dead code (§3).
2. **Rebuild the inputs (the real work):** `derive_assumptions.py` from stored IndianAPI history with sector betas (C1). This is what makes the DCF independent and accurate.
3. **One valuation, precomputed:** add the `Valuation` table, compute once, read everywhere; retire the per-request DCF and the consensus overlay as the anchor (C4, C5).
4. **Finish the financial-sector pipeline:** populate NII/provisions for banks & NBFCs (C6 #2, #4).
5. **Validation layer + a small test suite** (§4, §5.3).
6. **Historical valuation bands** (§5.4) as the first net-new feature.
